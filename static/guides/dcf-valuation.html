Discounted cash flow valuation

A discounted cash flow (DCF) model estimates value from future cash generation and a discount rate. In StatementIQ, those future figures are your assumptions, kept separate from the reported historical baseline.

Build from reported figures

Open the company workspace and find the forecast and DCF section. A model needs a coherent baseline and sufficient supported statement inputs. Enter the five annual forecast periods and explicit discount-rate, terminal-growth, reinvestment, and equity-bridge assumptions. No forecast is an observed company result.

The model links forecast statements, checks funding needs, and provides downloads for reviewing the assumptions outside the website. Availability depends on the company's data and model applicability.

From operations to enterprise value

The model discounts annual free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) at the weighted average cost of capital (WACC), using end-of-year discounting.

FCFF = after-tax operating profit + depreciation and amortization − capital expenditure − increase in operating working capital

It adds the present value of the explicit forecasts to the present value of the terminal business. In the terminal period, reinvestment is linked to growth and return on invested capital (ROIC):

Terminal reinvestment rate = terminal growth ÷ terminal ROIC
Terminal value = next-year normalized FCFF ÷ (WACC − terminal growth)

WACC must exceed terminal growth. For positive terminal growth, terminal ROIC must also exceed that growth rate. Unsupported inputs or unresolved funding gaps can prevent a usable valuation.

From enterprise value to equity value

The equity bridge adds the explicitly entered eligible cash and non-operating assets, then deducts reported debt and other entered claims. A per-share result uses the supported reported point-in-time common share count at the baseline date; weighted-average EPS shares are not substituted.

Choose bridge items carefully so cash, debt, or other claims are not counted twice. Check whether the available baseline and claims actually fit the company's capital structure.

Use scenarios, not a single promised price

Small changes in WACC, sustainable margins, growth, and reinvestment can change the result substantially. Compare conservative and optimistic assumptions, and inspect how much of the valuation comes from the terminal period.

A DCF is conditional on its assumptions. It is not a guaranteed share price, and an operating-company FCFF model is not automatically suitable for a bank, insurer, or REIT. See model scope and limitations. For background on sustainable terminal reinvestment, see Aswath Damodaran’s valuation notes at NYU Stern.
