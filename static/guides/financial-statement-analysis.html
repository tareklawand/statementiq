How to analyze financial statements

Financial statement analysis connects a company’s earnings, resources, obligations, and cash generation. Start with the reporting period and source, then read the statements together.

Start with the period

Check whether you are viewing a quarter, a full year, or trailing twelve months (TTM). Match the currency and units before comparing figures. In StatementIQ, the company header identifies the selected statement periods and source checks.

A latest-quarter balance sheet can be paired with TTM income and cash-flow figures for analysis, but the dates describe different things. Do not treat one quarter of revenue as a full-year result.

Read the statements together

Income statement
Follow revenue through expenses to profit. Compare margins over time and investigate material changes.
Balance sheet
Review assets, liabilities, and equity at the reporting date. Inspect near-term obligations, cash, and debt.
Cash flow statement
Separate operating, investing, and financing flows. Compare cash generation with reported earnings and investment needs.

Changes in shareholders’ equity and the notes to the accounts add context that summary tables cannot fully capture.

Connect the numbers

In the workspace, use Financial statements for reported rows, common-size analysis, and period changes. Use Ratio diagnostics for liquidity, leverage, profitability, efficiency, and valuation ratios. The executive overview summarizes the available evidence and the model's coverage.

Ask what caused a movement before assigning a meaning to it. Higher cash can come from borrowing; stronger earnings can coexist with weaker collections. One percentage rarely explains the whole business.

Finish with the filing

Read the notes, management discussion, and risks in the full annual or quarterly filing. The SEC’s guide to financial statements is a useful starting point.
