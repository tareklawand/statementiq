Data and methodology

Every analysis needs a source, a reporting period, and a clear distinction between reported facts and calculations. This page explains how StatementIQ handles those differences.

Where the figures come from

Market data: Yahoo Finance supplies prices and available valuation and company-profile fields. Quote timestamps matter; prices are not taken from SEC filings and are not guaranteed to be real-time.

Company statements: standardized provider statements are checked against the latest supported SEC reporting period. When those statements are missing or behind, the analyzer attempts a direct import of supported consolidated US-GAAP facts from SEC Company Facts. Custom-tag-only disclosures and unsupported reporting formats may remain unavailable.

Evidence: source labels, statement dates, filing references, and validation messages appear in the company workspace. Numeric cross-checks cover comparable facts, not every disclosure in the full filing.

New filings and reporting periods

Analyses use short-lived caches. A fresh source fetch checks for a newer supported SEC periodic filing and rebuilds the calculations from the selected statements. Scheduled filing checks also monitor companies in the saved public evidence archive. This is not an instant feed for every listed company: publication, provider updates, and SEC fact availability can arrive at different times.

The balance sheet is a point-in-time statement. Income and cash-flow calculations use the available trailing-twelve-month period, or a labelled annual period when a complete trailing period cannot be constructed. Review the displayed dates before comparing results.

How the financial-health screen works

The operating-company screen groups evidence into five components: liquidity, solvency, profitability and efficiency, cash-flow quality, and growth and per-share resilience. A headline score requires at least 80% of the model's expected weighted inputs and evidence in all five components.

Available scored inputs are combined using the model's weights. Missing inputs are disclosed; they are not fabricated. Metrics marked Context only do not affect the score because their interpretation does not have one consistently favorable direction. Valuation multiples feed a separate valuation screen, rather than being treated as financial strength.

Company type affects applicability. Utilities have adjusted leverage and coverage ranges. Banks, insurers, and REITs require specialist evidence; a general-company health score is withheld when those required inputs are not available. Unverified classification, material source disagreement, or an unresolved newer filing can also hold back a headline score.

Unavailable values and forecasts

N/A means an input or applicable result is unavailable. N/M means a calculation would not be meaningful, such as some multiples with a non-positive denominator. Neither is a substitute for zero.

The DCF model uses explicit assumptions entered by the researcher. Forecasts and sensitivity results are model outputs, not reported company figures or promised future prices. Downloads preserve the assumptions for review; there is no shared account-based research workspace.

Check the underlying evidence

Use the full filing to review accounting policies, debt terms, contingencies, segment details, and management commentary. Automated source checks are not an independent financial audit.
